The central claim

Good futures work separates evidence, inference, and imagination.

FACTWhat we can observe, measure, reproduce, or document in the present.
EXTRAPOLATIONA causal claim about how present conditions could produce a future state.
INVENTIONA premise we deliberately introduce to explore consequences that present evidence cannot establish.

The problem is not speculation. The problem is speculation disguised as fact.

Evidence must match the claim

A longer time horizon needs more humility, not more confidence.

1OBSERVEDMeasured capability, adoption, cost, resource use, or outcome under known conditions.
2REPLICATEDSimilar findings across methods, populations, settings, or independent teams.
3MECHANISTICA credible explanation links causes, constraints, and effects.
4PROJECTEDA model or trend extends beyond observed data with explicit assumptions.
5SPECULATIVEA premise explores what might follow if something genuinely new appears.

Socratic pause

Which sentence sounds rigorous but actually hides the most uncertainty?

A“A benchmark doubled in one year.”What was measured? Did the test change? Does it transfer to real work?
B“AI will automate science.”Which parts of science? At what reliability? Under whose authority?
C“Imagine a city whose infrastructure negotiates with residents.”The invention is obvious, so its assumptions are easier to question.

Second-order effects

The first effect changes the conditions that produce the next one.

AI LOWERS A COSTGenerating a design, diagnosis, forecast, lesson, or simulation becomes cheaper.
DEMAND EXPANDSMore people use the service, new use cases appear, and expectations rise.
BOTTLENECK MOVESValidation, physical production, expert review, energy, trust, or regulation becomes scarce.
ROLES CHANGENew expertise, institutions, markets, dependencies, and inequalities appear.

Strategic response

People do not remain frozen while AI changes around them.

ADAPTlearn new skills, change routines, develop workarounds, and redefine expertise
COMPETElower prices, differentiate, acquire rivals, control infrastructure, or create standards
RESISTrefuse, organize, litigate, regulate, sabotage, or build alternatives
APPROPRIATEuse the technology for purposes its designers did not anticipate

A future with passive humans is usually a weak sociotechnical future.

Rebound and scale

Efficiency can reduce the cost per task while increasing total use.

PER-UNIT IMPROVEMENTA model uses less compute, a robot wastes less material, or a digital twin improves system efficiency.
+
MORE UNITSCheaper access increases demand, more actors participate, new markets form, and total resource use can still rise.

The rebound principle is familiar from energy economics, but it applies more broadly. When creation, prediction, or experimentation becomes cheaper, the total amount produced may grow dramatically.

In AI futures, this matters for compute, content, experiments, surveillance, decisions, and automated actions.

Path dependence

Early choices can become hard to reverse even when better options appear.

EARLY CHOICEA platform, metric, data format, model, vendor, or legal definition wins initial adoption.
COMPLEMENTSTraining, contracts, infrastructure, workflows, and norms accumulate around it.
SWITCHING COSTExit becomes expensive, risky, politically difficult, or technically incompatible.
LOCK-INThe future inherits a decision that once looked temporary.

The novelty test

A future problem is genuinely new when the system changes the structure of the problem.

01NEW ACTORDoes an autonomous model, robot collective, digital twin, or synthetic organization gain a role that does not exist now?
02NEW RELATIONDo people, institutions, machines, or environments depend on one another in a new way?
03NEW SCALE OR SPEEDDoes machine-scale volume, coordination, memory, personalization, or action change what governance is possible?
04NEW SCARCITYDoes abundance in one resource make verification, legitimacy, attention, embodiment, energy, or exit newly scarce?
05NEW MORAL PATIENTDoes the world create serious claims about who or what deserves protection, representation, or care?

Synthesis

The goal is not to be right about 2045. It is to become harder to surprise in 2026.

SEE MORENotice weak signals, hidden infrastructure, institutional friction, and actors outside the dominant story.
THINK CAUSALLYMake mechanisms, dependencies, feedback, strategic response, and uncertainty explicit.
CHOOSE BETTERPreserve options, test robust strategies, widen participation, and shape more preferable futures now.

Futures are not destinations waiting for us. They are ways of organizing present attention and action.

The closing test

When someone shows you an AI future, ask whether the world around the technology is as carefully built as the technology itself.

01What is fact, what is extrapolation, and what is deliberate invention?

02Which causal pathway, dependencies, feedback loops, and bottlenecks make it plausible?

03How do people, institutions, markets, culture, and infrastructure respond?

04Who gains capacity, who becomes dependent, and who can refuse or appeal?

05What genuinely new problem appears, and what better future could we build?
